# Erdős Problem 1134

## Statement and scope

Let `A` be the least subset of the natural numbers containing `1` and closed
under `x ↦ 2x + 1`, `x ↦ 3x + 1`, and `x ↦ 6x + 1`.
[Problem 1134](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1134) asks whether `A` has
positive lower density.

`Erdos1134.not_erdos_1134` proves that it does not. The import also retains
`lower_density_zero` and the stronger quantitative estimate
`erdos_set_sublinear_bound`: there is a positive `C` such that
`|A ∩ [0,N]| ≤ C * N^(19/20)` for every natural `N` (including zero).
It does **not** formalize the sharper exponent approximately `0.900626`
reported on EPC, nor the open variants with different generators.

Membership in `A` is an inductive predicate with exactly the seed and three
closure rules. `ErdosSetA.smallest` proves minimality, and `ErdosSetA.pos`
proves positivity. Consequently, using `[0,N]` in the density definition
gives the same count as the problem's `[1,N]`; zero is not an extra element.

## Source, authors, and version

[Ashvin's EPC comment, 19 June 2026](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1134#post-7068)
attributes the formalization to **AxiomProver** and links
[`AxiomMath/erdos-public/Erdos/Erdos1134/solution.lean`](https://github.com/AxiomMath/erdos-public/blob/3ccf48c78b9df4aa26e1b2f90058bdd3f61da1ab/Erdos/Erdos1134/solution.lean).
This import pins repository commit
**`3ccf48c78b9df4aa26e1b2f90058bdd3f61da1ab`**. The source was published in
`8c05a325f5b5cfa7a5eeb2de53337a51cf1a4067`; the pinned commit adds build files.

The historical informal argument is due to **D. J. Crampin and A. J. W.
Hilton**, as reported by EPC and in the introduction of
[On orbit sets generated by semigroups of one-dimensional affine functions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0723086926000125).
The formal proof uses their nontrivial relation between affine compositions
to obtain canonical words, then bounds a Dirichlet series and applies a
counting estimate. The chosen exponent is `19/20`.

The source README identifies the artifacts as generated by AxiomProver.
No individual human formal proof author is identified. Ashvin announced
the result, and Evan Chen authored the publication commits; those facts
alone do not establish individual proof authorship, so neither is entered
as a formal author in `sources.yaml`.

The original toolchain is **Lean 4.27.0** and the original Mathlib revision is
**`a3a10db0e9d66acbebf76c5e6a135066525ac900`**. The port targets
**Lean/Mathlib 4.33.0**. The MIT license, including **Copyright (c) 2026 Axiom
Math**, is preserved in the Lean headers and `Erdos1134/LICENSE`.

## Layout and verification

`Definitions.lean` defines the generated set and lower density.
`Canonical.lean` constructs canonical operation words and the injection
used in counting. `Dirichlet.lean` proves summability and the sublinear
bound. The root file derives density zero and the negative answer.

The independent Comparator challenge contains only the inductive set,
the lower-density definition, and the negative result.

The port updates renamed APIs, makes the empty-list witness in the
zero-length series explicit, and removes redundant tactics and unused
positivity hypotheses. Proof resource limits are local to the three
infinite-series arguments that retain them.

`lake build ErdosProblems.Erdos1134 Erdos1134` passes with no solution
warnings. The challenge emits its intentional placeholder warning, and
unrelated dependency checkout notices remain. The negative answer,
density-zero theorem, and sublinear bound print only `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` as axioms.

After exporting the challenge and solution with `lean4export`,
Comparator's `compareAt` accepted the final statement and definitions,
and `checkAxioms` accepted the final theorem with those three permitted
axioms. Fresh Lean kernel replay of the exported solution also passed.

The full Linux sandbox/Nanoda runner is unavailable on this host. These
checks use the local Comparator APIs and Lean kernel; Nanoda remains
enabled in the configuration for the standard Linux runner.
