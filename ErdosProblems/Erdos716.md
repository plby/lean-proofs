# Erdős Problem 716

[EPC](https://www.erdosproblems.com/716) ·
[formalization comment](https://www.erdosproblems.com/716#post-7096)

## Statement and authorship

`Erdos716.erdos_716` proves the Ruzsa–Szemerédi (6,3)-theorem:
the extremal number of three-uniform hypergraphs avoiding three distinct
edges on at most six vertices is `o(n²)`.

A hypergraph is a finite set of finite vertex sets, with every edge having
cardinality three. `ThreeEdgesIn6` requires three pairwise distinct edges
whose union has at most six vertices; `ex3` maximizes the edge count among
hypergraphs without this configuration. For `n ≥ 6`, this is exactly avoidance
of EPC's family of all three-uniform hypergraphs on six vertices with three
edges, allowing isolated vertices. Any distinction for smaller `n` is
irrelevant to the asymptotic theorem.

The informal theorem is due to **Imre Z. Ruzsa and Endre Szemerédi** (1978).
**JoshuaB** posted the formalization generated by **Aristotle**. No fuller
human name was supplied. The proof applies the existing triangle-removal and
tripartite-graph machinery formalized in Mathlib by **Yaël Dillies and
Bhavik Mehta**; their work is imported, not copied or attributed to Aristotle.

## Provenance and port

The complete source was recovered from the compressed Lean editor URL in
EPC comment 7096. Its `project=mathlib-v4.28.0` parameter establishes the
original Lean/Mathlib version. The complete URL is preserved under
`JoshuaB_716` in `data/urls.yaml`; no separate Mathlib commit was supplied.
The comment also links Aristotle result
`a876356c-15d8-4d24-b51f-001f57955616`. The editor link, rather than the
inaccessible dashboard, supplied the imported proof.

SHA-256 of the decoded original source:
`e3cab0fd07a96b7ca75e6123376c30120fbad76beffed9fb50fa98d78b2e0156`.
No license was supplied with that source.

The port separates `Definitions` and `Construction`, uses namespace
`Erdos716`, and renames the final source theorem `ex3_isLittleO` to
`erdos_716`. The independent Comparator challenge imports only Mathlib and
repeats the two definitions and the final theorem.

The port removes unused global scope openings, makes classical instances
local, updates the negation tactic, supplies the triangle-removal instance
explicitly, and replaces one failed automated cardinality bound by the
inclusion-exclusion identities for finite unions. The concluding norm
conversion is expressed directly using Mathlib's norm lemmas.
